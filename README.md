## 🔬 Core Scientific Innovations

### 1. The Primordial Vortex Ansatz
[cite_start]The model moves beyond the standard Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric by introducing a vector potential $\mathcal{B}_i$[cite: 5]. [cite_start]This represents an antisymmetric field coupled to curvature, effectively adding a rotational "vortex" component to the fabric of spacetime[cite: 5].

### 2. Spacetime Vortex Gravity (SVG)
[cite_start]Unlike standard General Relativity, SVG treats gravitational phenomena as emergent effects of these vortical dynamics[cite: 5]. [cite_start]In the context of cosmogenesis, this vortex is a remnant of the "Parent" black hole's collapse, which seeds the "Child" universe with rotational energy[cite: 5].

### 3. The "Hidden Collapse" Energy Exchange
[cite_start]The framework utilizes the **Hidden Collapse** hypothesis to describe how energy is exchanged between different spacetime domains[cite: 5]. [cite_start]This energy-exchange mechanism governs the dissipation of the vortex over cosmic time[cite: 5].

## 🧮 Mathematical Framework

### Modified Einstein Field Equations
[cite_start]The standard Einstein equations are augmented with a vorticity tensor component[cite: 5]:
$$G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G (T_{\mu\nu} + T_{\mu\nu}^{vortex})$$

### The Effective Vortex Fluid
[cite_start]The vortex is modeled as a phenomenological effective fluid with a specific energy density $\rho_{v}(a)$ and pressure $P_{v}$[cite: 5]. Its evolution is governed by:
* [cite_start]**Conservation Laws:** How the vortex scales as the universe expands[cite: 5].
* [cite_start]**Vortex Dissipation:** A decay function that reduces vortex influence as the universe matures[cite: 5].

### Modified Friedmann Equations
[cite_start]The expansion rate $H(a)$ is directly influenced by the vortex density[cite: 5]:
$$H^2(a) = \frac{8\pi G}{3} \left( \rho_m + \rho_r + \rho_\Lambda + \rho_v(a) \right)$$

## 📈 Resolving the Hubble Tension

[cite_start]The model provides a dual-phase resolution to the $H_0$ crisis[cite: 5]:

1.  [cite_start]**Early-Universe Phase:** High vortex dominance in the primordial era alters the sound horizon and the scale of the CMB, leading to a lower "inferred" Hubble rate when using standard $\Lambda CDM$ assumptions[cite: 5].
2.  [cite_start]**Late-Time Phase:** As the vortex dissipates (decays), the local expansion rate converges to a higher value, matching the observations from Cepheids and Supernovae (SH0ES)[cite: 5].

[cite_start]This naturally produces the "split" in expansion rates without requiring "new" dark energy or modified gravity constants[cite: 5].
